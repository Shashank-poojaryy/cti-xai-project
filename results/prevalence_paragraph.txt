Test-set prevalence and metric choice. The held-out test set preserves the real-world cardiomegaly prevalence of 4.05% (522 positives among 12903 images) under a strict patient-independent split with zero patient overlap; the class distribution was deliberately left unaltered (no oversampling or test-set rebalancing). At this prevalence the F1 score at a fixed 0.5 threshold is mathematically constrained: precision is bounded by the base rate, so a low F1@0.5 reflects the operating point and class distribution rather than weak discrimination. We therefore report prevalence-robust metrics as the headline. Area under the ROC curve (AUC-ROC) is prevalence-invariant, and area under the precision-recall curve (AUPRC, i.e. average precision) is the appropriate summary for imbalanced detection because its no-skill baseline equals the prevalence (0.0405). All three models far exceed this baseline; the best model (ResNet50) achieves AUPRC = 0.305, 7.5 times the no-skill value, while maintaining AUC-ROC = 0.897. Selecting a task-appropriate operating point recovers clinically useful performance: at the F1-optimal threshold F1 roughly doubles relative to 0.5, and at high-sensitivity thresholds (recall >= 0.90, suited to screening) the models retain high specificity. A controlled demonstration confirms the effect is distributional: when the test prevalence is artificially increased by subsampling negatives (for illustration only), F1 rises monotonically while AUC-ROC stays essentially constant - direct evidence that the low headline F1 is a prevalence artifact, not a deficiency of the model or the patient-independent split.
